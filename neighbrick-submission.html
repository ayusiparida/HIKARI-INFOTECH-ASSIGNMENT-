C

States inventory

20 states across both journeys. S marks the three designed as frames.

Pending

  • Brick purchase processing (2 min to 24 h), pledge lined up. Shown on A3.
  • Purchase still processing at the goal deadline. Pledge isn't made; if confirmed later, Bricks land in her balance.
  • Pledge inside its 24-hour cancel window, with "Cancel until …" time, ending early at the deadline.
  • Goal ended, Bricks being returned (up to 1 hour): "Your Bricks are on their way back".
  • Payout Submitted / Processing. Shown on B2.
  • Proof of use due: days left shown to Joseph and supporters; Joseph can't start a new goal.

Error

  • Purchase not confirmed after 24 hours; bank refund within 5 working days.S3
  • Ritu cancels or the payment is declined in the UPI app. Back on A2, nothing charged, no pledge.
  • Shop payout failed: "UPI ID not found".S2
  • Bank payout failed: "bank temporarily unavailable". Money safe; Joseph retries, no details to change.
  • No internet on the goal page or when returning from the UPI app: last known status shown with "Checking again when you're online".

Empty

  • Zero Bricks balance: A2 shows the full amount as a pack choice.
  • Goal with no supporters yet: "Be the first to pledge", no empty list.
  • Goal without an invoice: only "To my bank account" offered on B1.
  • Proof not posted yet (supporter view): "Joseph has until [date] to post proof".

Edge

  • Goal ended, Bricks returned (water cooler).S1
  • Goal past 100% before the deadline (D3): "Target passed, confirmed at the deadline" plus the extra-money line.
  • A cancellation drops the goal back below 100%: bar and wording update, no "reached" to take back (D2).
  • Near the 100-Brick cap: if she has already pledged 90, the stepper stops at 10 more and says why.
  • WhatsApp link opened after the goal has ended: result shown, pledge button removed.
D

Written section

About 750 words. Limits: D1 to D4 under 40 words each; Q1 150, Q2 100, Q3 80, Q4 100, Q5 120.

Assumptions

  1. Dates are illustrative: Journey A starts Mon 7 Dec 2026, 7:40 pm; the reading-corner deadline is Thu 10 Dec, 9:00 pm. Working days skip weekends and bank holidays.
  2. Ritu pledged 15 Bricks to the water cooler before Journey A. After Journey A she holds 2 Bricks, so S1 shows 2 + 15 = 17 Bricks.
  3. A shop payout is one payout: Neighbrick pays the shop first and sends the remainder only once the shop is paid, so a shop failure means nothing has moved.
  4. Neighbrick can show the name registered to a UPI ID before sending, as UPI apps do.
  5. While a lined-up pledge waits, Ritu's existing 12 Bricks are set aside for it.

Open decisions

D1. Both, rupees first. Targets, progress, fee and payouts are rupees only. Bricks appear where Bricks move (balance, packs, pledge), always paired: "30 Bricks (₹300)". Ritu thinks in rupees; Bricks are the bookkeeping.

D2. Only at the deadline. Before then, 100% or more reads "Target passed, confirmed at the deadline", with no celebration, because a cancellation within 24 hours can still pull it back below.

D3. A plain line above the pledge button: "Pledges above ₹12,000 also go to Joseph for this goal", followed by the organiser's note on what extra buys, if written. No redirect to other goals.

D4. Yes. One confirm lines the pledge up; it goes through when Bricks arrive and counts for nothing before. If unconfirmed in 24 hours or by the deadline: no pledge, her 12 Bricks freed, bank refunds ₹200.

Q1. The visibility decision

Neighbours see the supporter count and first names with an initial, listed A to Z with no times so they can't be matched to record timestamps. Amounts are never shown. On A2, "Show my name" is on and switchable to "A neighbour" before paying.

The public record stays quiet but findable, first met on A1 under "How your money is protected": "Every pledge and payout is written in a public receipt book that anyone can check. It shows amounts and times, never names or phone numbers." Link: "See this goal's receipt book". No "blockchain" in the main UI.

Losers: small givers who miss the default, and checkers like Mr. Rao, one tap deeper. Mitigation: the choice comes before paying and can be changed.

Relied on: 64% vs 45%, 38% hiding at ₹50 or less, 4% opens, 11 crypto questions. I'd switch to hidden-by-default if over 35% opt out.

Q2. Bricks back, not rupees

Before she pays anything: on A1's "If the goal is missed" box, then on A2's confirm sheet, beside the ₹200 purchase. That is when she can still choose less or nothing; learning it afterwards would repeat her "nobody knew where the money went" experience. S1 leads with her outcome in her terms, "15 Bricks (₹150) are back", the exact return time, "No fee was taken" and her new balance. "As we said when you pledged" restates the rule rather than revealing it, and the screen offers next steps and the receipt-book entry, not a dead end.

Q3. The fee

Both. Supporters see one line on A1 and again on A2's confirm sheet before paying: "If the goal succeeds, Neighbrick keeps 7% of the final total. You pay nothing extra." Joseph sees it on B1 before choosing a payout, with the working: ₹885.50 rounds up to ₹886. Showing it before commitment avoids a surprise deduction, and the exact figure lets Joseph tell neighbours where the ₹886 went.

Q4. The riskiest moment

Returning from the UPI app to "processing": ₹200 has left Ritu's bank and no Bricks show, echoing her last collection. Response: she confirms everything before the hand-off, so nothing is left to do; A3 shows what's done and waiting, the 24-hour rule, the refund and her UPI reference; push and SMS when it resolves; S3 keeps those promises. Not reduced: we can't speed up a slow bank, a failed purchase still takes up to 5 working days to refund, and a long delay near the deadline can cost her the pledge.

Q5. Keep up to three

Keep 3, photo updates: answers "where did the money go?" (29% of support messages) before proof is due.

Keep 4, deadline reminder: all-or-nothing makes the last day decisive; only people who opened the goal.

Keep 5, Hindi and Marathi: Pune colonies include neighbours less at ease in English.

  • 1 can wait: shows who gave next door; 38% of small givers already hide names.
  • 2 can wait: needs moderation, and neighbour disputes would play out in public.
  • 6 can wait: changes totals, fee and payout maths, and needs association agreements.
  • 7 can wait: doesn't fit fixed goals with deadlines and all-or-nothing returns.
  • 8 can wait: only 4% opened the record, and the word "verified" invites the crypto question.
